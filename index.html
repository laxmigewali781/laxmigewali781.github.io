About the Department

The Department of Computer Science sits within the Howard R. Hughes College of Engineering at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas. It is one of the fastest-growing departments on campus, and computer science students make up nearly half of the college's total enrollment. The department is recognized nationally and internationally for research in both theoretical and experimental computer science — including algorithm design, information retrieval, document analysis, computer graphics, computational geometry, networking, and internet security.

For international students, the department offers a close-knit academic community: small graduate seminars, faculty who are active, funded researchers, and dedicated academic and research laboratories that support both coursework and thesis or dissertation work.

How the Graduate Program Grew

The Master's and Ph.D. programs in Computer Science were built to let students study specialized areas in depth while making original research contributions. Over time, the graduate program expanded from a small set of course offerings into a full research program with its own laboratories, funded centers, and dedicated graduate faculty.

  1. Early growth inside the College of Engineering

    Computer Science began as part of UNLV's engineering programs and grew quickly alongside the university itself, adding dedicated faculty and its own graduate curriculum as demand for trained computer scientists in Las Vegas and beyond increased.

  2. Establishing the M.S. and Ph.D. tracks

    The department formalized graduate degrees leading to the M.S. and Ph.D. in Computer Science, with thesis, project, and course-only options for the master's degree so students could tailor their path toward research, industry, or further doctoral study.

  3. Building dedicated research infrastructure

    As the faculty's funded research grew, the department stood up in-house computer laboratories and research centers — including the Center for Information Technology and Algorithms (CITA) — giving graduate students hands-on access to high-performance computing, GPU resources, and specialized data sets for their research.

  4. A broader, more specialized graduate faculty

    The graduate faculty expanded into new specialties — cybersecurity, bioinformatics and machine learning, accessible computing, blockchain, and human-computer interaction — alongside the department's long-standing strengths in algorithms, document analysis, and computational geometry.

  5. Today's program

    The department now includes more than 25 faculty members and offers interdisciplinary graduate options, such as the M.S. in Data Analytics and dual degrees in Cybersecurity, alongside its core M.S. and Ph.D. in Computer Science — continuing to attract graduate students, including many international students, from around the world.

Graduate Faculty & Research Areas

The list below highlights a sample of the department's graduate faculty and the research each one is currently known for. Use it as a starting point when looking for a potential thesis or dissertation advisor — then reach out directly or through your graduate advisor to discuss open positions in their lab.

Dr. Laxmi Gewali

Professor & Department Chair

Designs and analyzes efficient algorithms, with a focus on the visibility properties of polygons, computational geometry, and planning collision-free paths — work that has been supported by NSF, the U.S. Department of Energy, and other federal sponsors.

Dr. Wolfgang Bein

Professor

Studies combinatorial optimization and online algorithms — methods for making good decisions when information arrives piece by piece — with applications ranging from resource and facility planning to broader questions in theoretical computer science.

Dr. Kazem Taghva

Professor

Works on information retrieval from noisy, OCR-scanned text, named-entity recognition, and database dependency theory; led a decades-long, federally supported effort to build one of the largest government document collections for legal discovery.

Dr. Andreas Stefik

Professor

Creator of Quorum, an evidence-based programming language designed to be accessible to blind and visually impaired learners; his lab studies programming-language usability, software engineering, and computer science education.

Dr. Mingon Kang

Associate Professor

Builds machine learning and deep learning methods for bioinformatics and big data, including interpretable and evidential deep learning models used to analyze genomic and clinical data with medical research partners.

Dr. Yoohwan Kim

Professor

Researches cybersecurity, blockchain, and cryptocurrency systems, including secure data-management architectures for Internet-of-Things devices built on private blockchain networks.

Dr. Fatma Nasoz

Professor

Works in human-computer interaction and affective computing, including artificial intelligence systems that recognize facial expressions and other signals to make computing more responsive to users.

Dr. Ju-Yeon Jo

Professor

Focuses on computer networks and cybersecurity, including software-defined networking and decentralized systems built on blockchain infrastructure.
